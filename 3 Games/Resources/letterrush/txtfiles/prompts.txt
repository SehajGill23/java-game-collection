
Best Experience with AI:
One of my best experiences with AI while writing this code was how it helped me set up a logical base structure. At the
beginning, I uploaded the PDF instructions for WordGame, NumberGame, and LetterRush into ChatGPT. Even though the
instructions were pretty straightforward, I asked it to create a clear outline of tasks for each game. ChatGPT broke
things down really well—like word matching for WordGame and scoring logic for NumberGame. While implementing WordGame
I had a huge bug in which I wasn't getting accurate high score from the text file, score.txt, such as upon creating
a new high score it would say "No new high score at this time". I tried debugging and after struggling with it for a
couple of hours, I gave up. I thought about pasting that class into ChatGPT to find out where the bug lies, GPT advised
 me to do further debugging and gave me instructions to do so. Eventually I was able to find where the issue was
arising from, the numbers from the text file were not able to parse into integer properly, hence messing up the whole
logic, giving inaccurate results.

For the Custom Game, though coming up with a unique idea for the custom game was a bit tough with AI, once I had my
idea, I was able to bring it to life with its help. For example, I wanted to add letter animations in my game and GPT
introduced me to AnimationTimer, an abstract class, and explained it in a really simple way. As AnimationTimer is not
the part of course outline under javaFX, I was wondering if I could use it, upon asking Jason about this I got approved
by him on its implementation. ChatGPT showed me how I could use it to refresh the screen and move letters smoothly.
That was a game-changer—once I understood how it worked, I felt way more confident adding animated features.

AI also helped with small improvements, like handling key events better and simplifying the game loop and logic.
Overall, while expressing creative ideas to AI was a bit tricky, its technical guidance made my code much cleaner, more
organized, and more functional than it would’ve been otherwise. Lastly, AI also helped me double-check the style
requirements. I made a checklist based on the style instructions provided by Jason, then pasted parts of my code into
ChatGPT and asked if it matched the checklist. That really helped me feel confident about my final version.



Worst Experience with AI:
One of my worst experience with AI came when, I was Initially thinking about the idea of what game to make. I gave it a
prompt "give me an idea of a unique game that is a mixture of 2 existing games, which makes it more fun". However, the
ideas given by ChatGPT was very vague, were hard to visualize and lacked creativity. After giving multiple prompts I
realized AI struggles to generate truly unique and creative concepts. Although, it can copy extremely well because of
its vast knowledge, but falls short on original creation. Eventually out of nowhere, an idea of collecting words
clicked into my mind, maybe because I tested word game recently, and came up with the idea of making letterRush, where
you use your cursor, to play with it.

While beginning to implement LetterRush game, I wrote the logic of the game in a Word document first, just to get
everything clear in my mind. After finishing that, I asked ChatGPT if the same logic could be built using JavaFX. It
said yes—but then completely changed the logic on its own. I had to go back and forth with it, writing around four
different prompts just to make it understand what I was actually trying to do. Even then, I had to guide it with
specific details to keep it on track. Though ChatGPT helped me a lot throughout this project—as I mentioned earlier in
my best experiences—I also had some frustrating moments. One of the worst was with the scoring logic. I had it working
around 90% correctly, but the bonus points weren’t adding up right. I asked ChatGPT to help figure out the issue since
I couldn’t spot it myself. It gave me an updated version of the class and pointed out a few possible issues. But after
implementing those changes, the score system dropped from 90% to around 60% functionality.I ended up discarding that
commit from Git and told ChatGPT to slow down and go step by step to find the issue more carefully.

Eventually, I got to a point where I thought the scoring logic was finally solid—it seemed to work 94% of the time. But
I was wrong. The logic held up only until I restarted the game using a restart button I had added in the game menu or
tried to proceed to the next level. After restarting, an alert message I had set to pop up when the player hits an
obstacle started triggering randomly. I brought up the new issue to ChatGPT, but it wasn’t able to properly solve it.
In the end, I had to sit down and rewrite the entire scoring logic by myself. Now, thankfully, it works properly.
